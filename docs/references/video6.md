1. ATTACKS / ANIMATION
- Structure: activation (one-frame burst) -> approach -> grab against the wall -> hard cut to a scripted cinematic cutscene (about 6 s) -> return to gameplay with the result applied (victim crumpled at the wall).
- The cutscene camera uses many short shots (2-12 frames each): extreme close-ups of accessories/faces, low angles, ground-level foot shots. Cuts are hard cuts or whip-through-black wipes.
- Pacing contrast: slow/static holds (4.93-6.93 s, about 30 frames of almost nothing) right before the hit make the 7.00 s strike land harder.
- The hit itself is not shown as 3D contact. It is replaced by impact frames plus a 2D manga sequence (about 2.5 s). This is a "show the impact abstractly" approach.
- Hit reaction: after the cutscene, the victim is placed slumped/kneeling at the wall base. Knock-down, no ragdoll limbs flying. Wall-slam dust at 4.47 s.

2. HIT VFX
- Activation: white starburst at the feet, red curved ribbon trails framing the screen, red triangle shards, white glints, ground smoke ring, translucent red dome. Peak on frame 1, decay over about 13-15 frames, with embers lingering about 1 s.
- Impact frames: alternating full-screen solids. Red beam on black (7.00), white-on-black speed spike (7.07), white beam on black (7.13-7.33), red-with-black silhouette (8.27, 9.93), pure black (8.33), red/black noise (10.00). Each lasts 1-2 frames.
- Manga/ink overlay: hatching that draws itself on, boiling line art, ink debris, a colour shift from black ink to red ink to signal escalation.
- Speed lines: full-screen horizontal streaks as a transition (9.87 s).
- Return: red fiery particles on the victim, a vertical red slash line on screen, a ground dust trail, a lingering red smear on the wall and a smoke column for 1+ s.
- No damage numbers, no hit markers. The UI is only the ability bar draining at the start and the victim's overhead HP bar. HUD hidden during the cutscene.
- Colour grading: the palette is restricted to red, black and white during the cinematic, which contrasts strongly with the neutral grey/green arena.
- Screen shake: hard to confirm at 15 fps. The rapid cuts and frame-to-frame jitter of the line art stand in for it.

3. GORE
- None visible. No blood, decals, pools or dismemberment. The red elements are energy/aura, stylised and glowing, not liquid. The victim survives visually intact (crumpled at the wall). Body persistence: the victim stays slumped at least 1 s after the return, and then the camera turns away.

4. SOUND (INFERRED) / PACING
- Likely sound sync points: the activation boom/whoosh at 2.00 s, a grab thud at about 3.5-4.1 s, a wall-slam crunch at 4.47 s, heavy footsteps at 4.93-5.7 s, silence or low drone during the 6.6-6.9 s hold, a big slash/boom at 7.00 s with sustained ringing through the white beam (7.13-7.33), impact cracks on each red flash (8.27, 9.93-10.00), and an explosion/rumble at 10.07 s.
- Intensity curve: spike (2.0 s) -> calm -> build (4.1-6.9 s slow and moody) -> extreme peak (7.0-10.0 s continuous flashes and stylised art) -> resolution (10.1-11.5 s smoke settling). About 6 s of cutscene inside an 11.5 s clip.

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS (for a realistic gritty gore game, adapt the structure, not the anime look)
- Finishers/grabs against a wall should trigger a short scripted cinematic (4-6 s), with hard cuts between 6-10 camera shots, each 2-12 frames (0.13-0.8 s) long.
- Put a static or slow "hold" shot of about 0.5 s right before the decisive hit. The contrast sells the impact.
- Use 1-2 frame full-screen impact frames (solid colour plus silhouette, or inverted) on the hit, alternating 3-5 times over about 1 s. For a gritty tone, use desaturated/high-contrast red-black-white rather than pure anime red.
- Ability activation: put everything on frame 1 (flash, burst, particles, ground dust ring, dome shockwave), then let it decay over about 15 frames (1 s), leaving lingering embers/smoke.
- Wall-impact dust/smoke cloud appears the frame the body hits the wall and lingers 1+ s. Leave residue (a smear or decal on the wall) after the move ends.
- Show the result on return to gameplay: the victim slumped at the wall base, with a ground drag trail to the impact point. In this project, that is where blood decals, wall splatter and limb loss should be added, since this reference has no gore.
- Use extreme close-ups (face, eyes, accessories) as character beats. In a gore game, use them for the victim's face or the wound at the moment of impact.
- A colour-palette shift during the cinematic (desaturate the world, push red) clearly separates "cinematic moment" from normal play.
- Hide the HUD during cinematics. Use no damage numbers. Feedback is purely visual and audio.
- Make sure the cutscene is server-triggered and both players are locked (the victim is pinned during the grab). Return control exactly where the cutscene ends.
- Uncertain: whether the 2D manga segment is a pre-rendered video/image sequence or in-engine (it looks like flipbook ImageLabels/decals on screen). For a realistic style, a desaturated film-grain slow-motion hit would be the equivalent.
