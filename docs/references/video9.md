VIDEO 9: SUMMARY
This is not a gore video. It is a 69 s showcase of a fan-made custom moveset for The Strongest Battlegrounds (TSB), "Sunken Hero" (water and anchor theme). The game's overlay text names the moves: "1st move: Anchorage", "2nd move: Riptide", "3rd move: Aweigh", "4th move: Wavebreaker", "Ult Start: Tidal Crash", "1st Ult Move: Tidal Surge". All of it is cartoon R6/blocky Roblox style. Every hit lands on a "Weakest Dummy" in the TSB beach map.

There is no blood, no dismemberment and no wounds anywhere in the video. Its value for us is the hit-feel and VFX language: white flash frames, shockwaves, rubble, camera work and a percentage damage readout. The bright blue water-magic colours do NOT fit a gritty realistic game. Copy the timing and layering, not the colours.

1. ATTACKS / ANIMATIONS
- Intro / ult transform (0.13–2.5 s): the character stands still, then a 4-point white star-glint flash appears for 2 frames (0.33–0.40 s). A blue-white swirling water bubble then wraps the body for about 2 s and dissolves over about 4 frames (2.40–2.53 s). The moment of change happens inside one flash frame.
- At 3.53 s a full-screen blue radial "speed-line burst" fills the screen for 1 frame. The camera then cuts to a dark vortex ring behind the player, with flying black cubes (debris) and a ground ring of light. A dark smoky aura lingers about 1.5 s and fades to normal lighting (5.0–5.4 s).
- M1 / basic combo (6.2–8.0 s, also 17.4–18.9 s and 34.9–37.9 s): there are 4 punches, each about 6–8 frames apart (roughly 0.45 s cadence). The anticipation is tiny, 1–2 frames of arm pull-back. Each damage tick (100→97→94→90 %, then 67→64→60 %) appears on the impact frame. The dummy flinches only slightly, which is a small stagger, not ragdoll.
- On the 4th hit of the combo (about 7.47 s) the camera shifts and the dummy is shoved sideways. At 8.0 s the player gets a white/desaturated tint for about 0.5 s. This reads as a "charged/empowered" state.
- Anchorage (8.6–12 s) is a multi-hit barrage. A blue splash/rings particle cloud wraps the target for about 2.7 s while white streak "slash lines" (thin and tapered, about 2–3 frames each) cut across it from many angles. The camera orbits slowly. The barrage ends (11.33 s) with a huge white rectangular slab/shockwave plane (a wall of water read as a solid white card) appearing over 1 frame. The dummy is launched backward into it and ends up sitting and ragdolled (11.87 s). The slab fades to translucent pillars over about 0.5 s.
- Grab / ground-pound follow-up (12–17 s): the player mounts the downed dummy and strikes. Each slam spawns large grey concrete slabs that are thrown up and tumble (12.73–13.2 s), plus a white horizontal speed-smear across the frame for 2–3 frames and dust clouds. Ground blue cracks/veins appear under them (14.5–16.3 s). The dummy is rag-dolled, bounced and kicked around. Rubble chunks stay on the ground for several seconds.
- Riptide (18.9–22 s): one full white flash frame (19.00 s) with a blue water jet, then an anchor prop appears in hand. A thin white line streak marks the swing. The anchor drags along the ground with orange sparks and embers at the floor contact (19.47–19.8 s). That is a nice physical detail: metal scraping stone gives orange sparks.
- Riptide hit (20.67 s): white flash-burst at impact, then a ground shatter of grey slabs and blue star glints. The camera sits low and is shaken during the slab eruption (21.0–21.5 s). Big slabs fill the foreground.
- Aweigh (23.2–26.6 s): a 1-frame white-screen flash (23.27 s), then the anchor is held horizontally behind the neck. White wind-crescent trails sweep around the body (wind-up of about 1 s). At 24.33 s there is another whiteout frame, then a vertical cylinder of white wind lines. At 25.0 s a blue-white energy dome with wind spirals appears. A "V"-shaped double line of grey rock spikes then erupts outward along the ground and stays about 1.5 s.
- Wavebreaker (27–34 s): a crouch wind-up of about 0.8 s. Blue ground ripple rings expand from the feet (28.0 s, again at 28.53 s), which is a charge telegraph. Release at 28.87 s: 2–3 frames of near-whiteout radial burst, a big white crescent shockwave and grey dust. The player then dashes along the ground leaving a blue water/flame trail and white crescent rings (29.2–30.7 s). They land in front of the dummy and burst rubble up (31.4 s). Rocks then erupt from below and juggle the dummy (32.2–33.5 s), with a small blue electric glyph on it.
- Second hit (33.73 s): a full-white flash for 1 frame, then a white halo ring overhead and a horizontal lens-flare streak through the target (34.2–34.4 s). Dark scorch/smoke is left on the ground.
- Tidal Crash (ult, 38.3–53 s): a long, slow cinematic. The player raises both arms (about 1.5 s held pose) and the camera pulls back very far (40–42 s). White water ripple rings spread from the player (42.4–44 s), then a huge dark-blue whirlpool/abyss fills the arena, rimmed by bright water. Lightning-like white/blue streaks fly across. The whirlpool goes black (49.3 s) with a glowing white orb at the player's hand. The arena stays dark for about 2 s and then returns to normal (51.9 s). It is pure spectacle with no target. Total about 13 s, far too long for a PvP game but useful as an "ultimate activation" mood reference.
- Tidal Surge (ult move, 53.9–66.6 s): the player pumps fists for about 1.5 s (power-up pose), then dashes in. At 56.5 s a giant vertical blue-white crescent water-slash comes with an extreme close-up camera on the dummy's face. The damage text is huge in red, "100%", filling the frame corner. Orange sparks and embers fly on contact (56.93–57.6 s).
- Then a sequence of beats, each with its own reaction:
  - A 1-frame white slab flash (58.27 s) and the dummy is knocked down, sliding.
  - A blue water-splash burst (58.9–59.5 s) and a white ring around the dummy.
  - A slab eruption (59.8–60.1 s) and the dummy is thrown up, cartwheeling.
  - A grab and repeated knees: the dummy's ragdoll limbs flail (60.5–61.2 s).
  - At 61.27 s a white horizontal flash with black speed slashes (2 frames), then a long white wind trail (61.4–61.8 s). Both stand locked (61.9–62.4 s), which reads as a grapple hold.
  - A spin throw with white ground rings (62.6–62.9 s).
  - A launch at 63.9 s: vertical blue lines, the camera low, water splash, then the dummy flies away with dust and smoke.
- Finale (65.07–65.9 s): a cylindrical white splash dome around the downed dummy with the anchor stuck in the ground, and embers rising. At 65.67 s a full-screen orange/yellow explosion fireball holds for about 3 frames, then dark smoke with floating red embers (65.8–66.2 s). The dummy then gets up (66.2 s; dummies regenerate). At 67.4 s the dummy dies and falls flat with a faint red glow outline. That is the only "death" signal, with no gore.

2. HIT VFX
- The core trick is the 1-frame full or partial WHITE FLASH on every big impact: 0.33, 3.53, 11.33, 19.00, 23.27, 24.33, 28.87, 33.73, 58.27, 61.27 and 65.67 s (that one is orange). They are always 1–3 frames, then the image cuts straight back to normal colours. This is what sells "impact" in this video.
- Speed lines: thin tapered white streaks (sometimes black) cross the target diagonally, lasting 2–3 frames, several per hit during barrages.
- Shockwaves: white flat rings or crescents on the ground, expanding over about 6–10 frames and fading. There are also vertical white cylinders of wind lines.
- Debris: large grey flat slab Parts thrown up and tumbling, plus smaller cubes. They have physics and stay on the floor for several seconds (around 5–10 s, then gone by the next scene). Dust is a beige/grey translucent smoke billow that lingers about 1–2 s.
- Sparks: orange/yellow ember sparks appear only when metal (the anchor) touches the ground or on the big ult hit. Embers float upward and fade over about 0.5–1 s.
- Camera: frequent hard cuts and angle changes per move, and close-ups during the ult hit (56.5–57.5 s, face fills the frame). Low ground camera during eruptions. Screen shake is visible during slab eruptions (blurred frames around 12.7 s, 21.1 s and 29.0 s). It is unclear whether there is an FOV punch, but the zoom-ins on big hits are evident.
- Colour grading: during ults the scene darkens or tints blue (5.0 s, 44–51 s), and the screen turns orange for the explosion. The player flashes white/desaturated when empowered (8.0–8.6 s).
- UI feedback: a big red bold "XX%" number above the dummy's head (TSB style). It updates on each hit frame (100→97→94→90…), floats and scales when a big hit lands (57 s: enormous "100%" in the frame). There is a small green HP bar under the name, and white combo-ish text plus a red "WRETCH!" chat-style kill feed line at the bottom (around 3.6–6 s). There are no floating damage-number popups other than that %, and no hit markers.

3. GORE
- None. No blood, no decals, no dismemberment, no wounds. The only "body after death" signal is the dummy collapsing flat on the floor with a faint red outline/glow (67.4–69.3 s), lying ragdolled for about 2 s, then respawning standing (69.4 s). For our game this video gives zero gore reference; use it for impact framing only.

4. SOUND CUES (inferred) AND PACING
- Sound almost certainly hits on each white flash frame: a heavy boom or whoosh. Each M1 damage tick gets a short punch. The water swirl and whirlpool phases imply sustained rushing-water and wind loops. Anchor scraping implies a metal grind with sparks. The finale fireball implies an explosion with a low-end tail.
- Pacing: the M1s are quick (about 0.45 s per hit). Specials run 2–4 s with about 0.5–1 s of telegraph (ground ripples, wind-up pose). Ults are long cinematic sequences (13 s for Tidal Crash, about 12 s for Tidal Surge) with many beats, each beat 0.3–0.8 s and separated by flash frames. The intensity curve goes flash, then busy particles, then calm hold, then the next flash.

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS (actionable)
- Put a 1–2 frame (about 33–66 ms) near-white or warm flash overlay on every heavy impact and finisher beat, then snap straight back. For our gritty style use a desaturated/white-hot flash at lower opacity (about 40–60 %), not a full whiteout, except on finishers.
- Pair each heavy impact with 2–4 thin tapered speed-line streaks across the victim that live 2–3 frames. Keep them white or near-black, not coloured, for a realistic look.
- On ground slams, spawn 3–8 physics debris slabs and chunks from the floor material that tumble and stay for 5–10 s, plus a beige dust billow that lingers 1–2 s. This is the most "weighty" element in the video and fits a realistic game well.
- Telegraph specials with 0.5–1 s of ground-ring or ripple build-up under the attacker, then release on a flash frame.
- Metal-on-stone contact (weapons dragged or hitting the floor) should emit orange sparks and embers that rise and fade over about 0.5 s. This is a cheap, realistic detail.
- Expanding flat ground shockwave rings (6–10 frames, alpha fade) on landings and slams. Keep them subtle and dusty for us.
- Light hits need only a short stagger (body jolt, no ragdoll). Heavy enders switch to ragdoll knockback with the victim sliding or tumbling, and a sit-up recovery after about 0.5–1 s.
- On big finisher hits, cut to a close-up camera on the victim for about 0.5–1 s, and pop the damage indicator large at the same moment.
- Damage feedback in the reference is a big red bold percentage that updates on the exact impact frame. If we show any number, tie it to the impact frame, not to the server ack delay (pre-play client-side and reconcile).
- Ult/finisher sequences are built as a chain of 0.3–0.8 s beats, each with a distinct reaction (launch, slam, grab, throw, explosion) and separated by flash frames. Keep the total to about 3–5 s for PvP. 12–13 s is too long.
- Brief scene darkening or tinting during an ultimate is effective. For us, desaturate and darken by about 20–30 % instead of blue.
- Do NOT copy the blue water/magic particle cloud or the anime aura. It clashes with a realistic gore game. Nothing in this video is a gore reference.
