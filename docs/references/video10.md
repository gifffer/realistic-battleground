VIDEO 10: overview and caveat
Video 10 is about 14.4 s long (216 frames at 15 fps). It is not a gore or fighting clip. It is a stylised, anime-like ultimate-ability VFX showcase. A white/lilac-haired R6 avatar with a purple butterfly/flower theme performs a long cinematic move on a dark night-sky baseplate, with blue clouds and a black floor. The right side of every frame shows a dark panel that looks like Roblox Studio's Explorer/Properties window, so this was most likely recorded in Studio as a VFX demo. There is no visible blood, no dismemberment, no HUD, no damage numbers and no hit markers. A target is barely visible: there may be a dummy or body on the ground around 7.8-9.5 s (small greenish and yellow specks there), but I am not sure. The useful material is choreography, camera work and VFX layering, not gore. Most of it is too magical and saturated for a gritty, realistic game, but the timing and camera techniques transfer well.

2. HIT VFX
- Impact flashes come in three strengths. (a) A local starburst at the hand or weapon tip, 1-2 frames (0.60 s, 4.67 s). (b) A full-screen white or pink bloom of 1-2 frames (7.73 s, 10.07-10.13 s, 12.33 s). (c) A single inverted black-and-white "impact frame" (2.93 s).
- Shockwaves: a circular ring or lens-distortion bubble (2.87 s, 13.00 s), and radial thin-ray starbursts (6.73-6.93 s, 12.20-12.27 s).
- Particles: constant drifting petals and butterflies in cyan, white and pink. Glassy shards. Long beam or streak particles with bright cores and coloured falloff (white core, then magenta, then a dark purple edge).
- Speed lines and radial motion-blur framing appear on the climax shots (5.80 s, 7.80-9.47 s, 9.53-10.00 s).
- Camera: mostly hard cuts between fixed or slowly moving shots. These include an over-the-shoulder close-up, a low wide shot, a back hero shot, a front shot and a ground-level barrage shot. No handheld shake is visible at 15 fps, but there may be subtle shake. The FOV appears to widen on the speed-line shots (a strong perspective "tunnel" at 9.53-10.00 s).
- Slow motion: not obvious. The pacing comes from holds and lulls rather than time-scaling.
- Colour grading: heavy. A dark navy and black base, fully saturated purple and magenta VFX, strong bloom, and a vignette on the explosion frames. One frame (12.67-12.80 s) shows rainbow chromatic aberration.
- UI: none (no health bar, damage numbers or hit markers).

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS (adapted for a gritty, realistic game)
- Put the main impact on a 1-2 frame flash (about 66-133 ms). It can be a full-screen white or tinted bloom, or a single inverted (negative) frame. Then drop straight back to a darker image. For a gritty game, use a desaturated white or a red-tinted flash at lower intensity instead of magenta.
- Precede every big hit with a 4-8 frame "lull" (darker, quieter, less VFX) so that the flash reads (as at 7.33-7.67 s before 7.73 s, and 11.13-12.27 s before 12.33 s).
- Use a single full-screen motion-smear frame to hide a camera cut on a finisher (0.73 s). This is cheap and very effective for cinematic kill cams.
- Finisher camera grammar: over-the-shoulder wind-up, then a hard cut to a low wide shot for the impact, then a close-up hero or back shot, then an aftermath shot. Each shot lasts about 0.3-1.7 s, and the shots are joined by cuts, not slow pans.
- Layer each impact as (1) a local starburst or spark at the contact point, (2) radial thin rays or speed lines, and (3) a ring or shockwave distortion. All three should fire on the same frame and fade within 3-6 frames.
- Rapid combos: show a short slash or streak trail every 2-4 frames along the strike line (4.8-5.7 s). For realistic fists, use dust and air-distortion streaks instead of neon trails.
- After a long move, hold the aftermath for 0.5-1 s with no new VFX (13.8-14.3 s, and 3.4-4.3 s), so the player registers the result. In a gore game, that is the moment to show the body and blood pool.
- Beam or streak particles should have a bright white core with a coloured falloff and a dark edge. That gradient reads well against dark scenes, and the same idea works for blood mist (a bright red core, a dark maroon edge).
- Add a radial speed-line or FOV-widen effect for 0.3-0.5 s before a finisher's contact frame (9.53-10.00 s). This creates tension without needing slow motion.
- Keep ultimate or finisher moves within about 3 s per phase. This one runs 14 s, which is far too long for a server-authoritative PvP fight. Borrow its rhythm and compress it.
- Do not use this video for gore, hit-reaction or ragdoll reference: none of that is visible.
