# Reference video 2

Analysed frame by frame at 15 fps. Contact sheets (3 s each, 9 frames per row, time on each tile): `docs/references/video2_*.jpg`.

VIDEO 2: what it shows
- About 29 s, vertical TikTok edit. 0-4.3 s is an edited title card ("i found the best [Roblox head with a blood drip] game", then "2025") over a blurred shot of bodies in blood. 27.5 s to the end is a blurred "GO WATCH the full video" outro. The gameplay runs from 4.4 to 27.5 s.
- The game is "BEFORE DAWN Engine v33", a first-person gun gore sandbox with blocky R6 dummies on a realistic, sunlit, slightly hazy city street. The player has white sleeves and holds an AK, then a shotgun (15 s), then a silver AK (19 s).
- There is NO melee in this clip: no punches, kicks or grabs. Everything is gunshot gore. The captions ("alright", "i guess we just shoot people", "help me", "BUDDY") were added by the editor, not the game.
- The source frames are only 240x427, so fine detail (bone vs flesh) is uncertain.

1. ATTACKS, ANIMATIONS, HIT REACTIONS
- Weapon feel: per-shot recoil kick on the viewmodel, plus a bright orange-yellow muzzle flash with spark streaks for 1-2 frames (10.60 s, 12.00 s, 15.33 s, 19.13 s, 21.07 s, 25.80 s, 26.27 s). The camera wanders loosely with the gun, with no cuts or cinematic angles.
- Hit reactions are physics-driven, not animated:
  - Rifle on a standing dummy (9.0-12.0 s): each hit makes the body flinch a few degrees and the arms flop. After the headshot the body stays standing for a few seconds, arms dangling and twitching at each new hit, then topples sideways and slumps onto the curb at about 11.8-12.1 s. It sags; it is never launched.
  - Shotgun at point blank (15.27-15.33 s): the dummy collapses in 3-4 frames (15.73 s it is crumpling, 15.93 s it is flat). It drops almost in place with very little knockback.
  - Silver AK (19.3-21.9 s): the body stays upright while being shot to pieces, slowly folds over, then the top half (torso and arms) tips off and falls behind a wall (21.6-21.9 s).
  - Shotgun on a group (25.8-27.5 s): the target goes from standing to shredded, and the torso folds and drops over about 1 s.
- Hitstop and slow motion: none visible in the game. Impacts happen at full speed. The punch comes from VFX and gore volume, not time manipulation.
- Camera: no visible FOV punch or zoom on hit. There may be slight recoil shake but it is hard to tell at this resolution. A glitch cut (14.33-14.40 s, a colour-bar screen) is an edit effect, not the game.

2. HIT VFX
- Muzzle and impact flash: a big warm orange fireball with spiky yellow sparks, lasting 1-2 frames (15.33 s and 25.80 s are the clearest). At 10.60 s and 26.27 s the flash lights up the victim, whose body goes bright for 1 frame. A light at the hit point is a useful trick.
- Hit marker: a small hollow circle ("o") stays on the body at the hit point (visible on most victims from 9.5 s on). This may be a crosshair or a hit indicator; I am not sure.
- Debris: small dark triangular fragments (cloth and flesh chunks) fly out and scatter on the ground (15.47-16.3 s, 22.2-24.3 s, 26.5-27.4 s). They stay on the floor afterwards.
- No damage numbers, no health bars, no screen tint, no vignette. Colour grading is warm, high-key and slightly overexposed, with strong sun bloom. That bloom makes the dark red blood stand out a lot.
- Dust and smoke: a light grey haze near the floor on impacts at 13.8 s and 26.6 s. Hard to tell whether it is smoke or a blood mist.

3. GORE (the core of the video)
- Wound decals that build up per hit:
  - Rifle at 9.0-11.9 s: first a red patch appears on the head (9.07 s). With each later hit the torso texture turns progressively redder: by 10.8 s the white shirt is about 50% red, and by 11.5-11.9 s the stripes have gone red and the chest is a wet red mess.
  - This is cumulative damage shown as decals and texture spreading at the hit points. It is the most "readable" gore feature in the clip.
- Bullet wounds open into holes: on the close-up of the fallen body (12.7-13.8 s) the torso shows lumpy, glossy dark-red crater geometry with bright red highlights, like crushed flesh with a wet specular shine. It is not a flat decal. The torso's blocky silhouette is broken by wound geometry.
- Headshot dismemberment:
  - The head is gone from about 9.73 s, after repeated head hits. The neck stump is a flat red disc with darker red and some lighter fragments; possibly bone, uncertain.
  - The silver AK case (19.6-21.5 s) is cleaner: the head turns red, then becomes a red-brown blocky stump while the torso splits. Inside (20.0-20.9 s) there is a pale grey-pink column, which looks like exposed spine or flesh layers, framed by red.
- Shotgun point blank (15.33-15.6 s): a huge blood cloud. A dense dark-red particle burst fills about 40% of the screen within 2 frames, then expands and fades over about 0.5 s.
  - Mixed in are fine droplets, larger globs and dark triangle chunks.
  - Blood also hits the camera and viewmodel as screen and gun splatter (15.4-15.6 s, red smears on the sleeve).
  - At 19.13-19.27 s a shot at a body on the floor sends a burst up into the lens, and the screen goes about 60% red for 2 frames.
- Blood colour: deep, saturated dark red (about #6a0000-#8b0a0a). The fresh mist is brighter red (#b01010) and the dried decals are darker brown-red. It is not neon or cartoon red.
- Floor decals and pools: after the shotgun kill a dark red pool spreads under the corpse, with spatter dots around it (16.0-17.0 s, still visible 2 s later).
- Directional streak decals: long thin streaks, splash shapes with "fingers" radiating away from the wound, painted on the floor and wall (17.3-18.5 s, around the corpse).
  - They look like velocity-stretched spatter decals: thin dark lines 1-2 studs long.
  - At 21.8-22.1 s blood hits the wall and roof edge behind the victim, leaving vertical streaks that run downward. They look like drip trails sliding down the wall, which suggests viscosity and dripping.
- Persistent chunks: after the wall kill (22.2-24.3 s) a large field of dark chunks and dots stays on the roof and street, with the red torso remains lying on the pavement below. Everything persists for at least 2-5 s on screen and likely much longer.
- Shooting a corpse: at 17.2-18.5 s the player walks up to the body on the ground and shoots it more.
  - Each shot opens new wounds: the plaid shirt goes from mostly intact to soaked red, with a crater at the chest/neck that has red ribbon-like strands, possibly intestines or flesh strips.
  - Each shot adds a fresh spray of streak decals on the ground. Corpses stay physical and damageable.
- Shotgun group kill (25.8-27.4 s):
  - The first frame is a fireball and sparks. Over the next 2-4 frames a cloud of large torn red "flakes" bursts out radially and upward, against the sky (25.93-26.07 s).
  - The victim behind is then a red skeleton-like ruin: dark red torso with lighter ribs and stripes (26.53-26.93 s), arms hanging. It slumps over about 0.8 s, and chunks and black cloth pieces scatter on the ground.
- Overall, dismemberment works by pieces: head removed, torso split or crumbled, limbs dangling. The stumps are dark red with lighter inner layers. The pieces keep simple R6 blocks but are re-skinned with gory textures.

4. SOUND (inferred) AND PACING
- Every muzzle-flash frame is a gunshot. Expect a heavy layered impact plus a wet splat on the same frame as the flash.
- A secondary wet "slosh" likely plays about 2-4 frames later, as the blood cloud expands and the decals land.
- The body-fall thud should come about 0.5-1 s later (15.9 s, 21.8 s, 27.3 s).
- Rhythm: single, deliberate shots about 0.5-1 s apart, not full-auto spam. Each shot's result is shown before the next.
- The intensity escalates through the video: rifle chip damage → shotgun explosion → shooting the corpse → wall splatter → multi-target. The camera always lingers 1-2 s on the aftermath (pools, streaks, chunks).

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Damage must accumulate visibly: every hit adds a red wound decal and darkens the body texture at the hit point. After about 5-8 hits the clothing is mostly red (9.0→11.9 s). Hit position must be readable.
- Wounds are wet 3D geometry, not flat decals: glossy dark-red crater meshes with high specular (wet look) and slightly brighter red highlights at the rim.
- Impact frame:
  - Frame 0: a 1-2 frame warm orange flash with spark streaks, plus a point light that lights the victim.
  - Frame 1-2: a dense dark-red particle cloud (mist + droplets + globs + small dark triangular chunks).
  - Over 0.3-0.6 s the cloud expands and fades.
- Velocity-stretched streak decals with "finger" splash shapes land on the floor/wall in the shot direction, 1-2 studs long. On walls they slide down as drips over about 1-2 s.
- A pool decal spreads under corpses over about 1 s. Spatter dots and chunks around it stay for a long time; never clean up on screen.
- Close-range blood hits the camera: a red splatter overlay on the screen and viewmodel, about 2 frames strong, fading over about 0.5 s. Reserve it for point-blank or heavy hits.
- Bodies are physics ragdolls with little knockback. They crumple roughly in place in about 0.4-0.6 s, or stay upright and twitch at each hit before slumping. No anime launches.
- Dismemberment by region: the head comes off and leaves a red stump with a lighter inner layer (bone/spine hint). Torsos can split or break into pieces. Limbs dangle. Severed parts keep the R6 block shape but get a gore texture.
- Corpses stay interactive: hits on a dead body open new wounds and new sprays. This sells the realism.
- Blood palette: dried decals about #5a0000-#7a0a0a, fresh spray up to about #b01515. Saturated but dark. Warm, bright, bloomy lighting makes it pop.
- No hitstop, no damage numbers, no slow motion. The impact comes from flash + blood volume + persistence. For our melee game, I would still add a 2-4 frame hitstop on heavy hits, since this gun clip offers no melee timing reference.
- Pacing: deliberate hits about 0.5-1 s apart, with a 1-2 s linger on the aftermath. Every kill leaves a lasting mess (chunks, streaks, pools) on the ground and walls.
