# Reference video 1

Analysed frame by frame at 15 fps. Contact sheets (3 s each, 9 frames per row, time on each tile): `docs/references/video1_*.jpg`.

VIDEO 1: WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS
Video 1 is about 21 s long. It is not a gore or fighting game. It shows no characters, no punches, no hit reactions, no blood and no dismemberment. It is a looping showcase of stylised toon (cel-shaded) spell VFX on a circular stone arena at night. Each element has its own glowing rune pad: fire explosion / mushroom cloud, ice crystals, water tentacle, green tornado, purple light pillar, fire geyser, lightning strike, pink/white slash, and a purple rock/meteor.

The camera does one slow continuous orbit or dolly around the arena and passes close to each effect. The video starts and ends on the same wide shot (0.0 s and 21.2 s), so it loops seamlessly.

The art style (flat orange shading, black outlines, bright pastel glow) does NOT fit a realistic, gritty game. What carries over is the timing and layering of the impacts: a flash, then the effect popping in, a hold, and a breakup. Sections 1 and 3 are therefore mostly "not shown".

1. ATTACKS / ANIMATIONS
- No characters, so there are no punch, kick, grab or finisher animations and no ragdoll, knockback or stagger.
- The only "attack-like" motion is the slash (15.60-16.13 s):
  - 15.60-15.73 s: a thin white crescent blade-trail arcs down onto the red pad. About 2-3 frames at 15 fps, so roughly 0.15-0.2 s of very fast travel with no slow-down.
  - 15.80 s: impact. A pink/magenta "X" double-slash appears instantly at full size. A white radial starburst of thin spark lines and some red streaks burst outward at the same moment.
  - 15.87-16.13 s: the starburst fades over about 4 frames. The white crescent trail lingers and slowly thins (about 0.3 s of follow-through).
  - The same move repeats at 17.60-18.33 s and 18.73-19.13 s with the same timing.
- Lightning (13.47-14.73 s), the anticipation-then-hit pattern:
  - 13.47-13.73 s: white zig-zag shock lines crawl along the ground.
  - 13.80-14.13 s: a dark scorch star with yellow cracks forms on the pad. Small yellow sparks rise from it (the build-up).
  - 14.20-14.40 s: a thick white-yellow bolt hits for about 3 frames. A bright bloom and radial sparks come with it.
  - 14.47-14.73 s: the bolt is gone and sparks fall. The black, star-shaped scorch decal with glowing yellow cracks stays.
- Anticipation: several effects start with a small glowing seed before the burst. Examples: the fire cone at 0.13 s and 3.07-3.13 s, and the white orb at 7.20-7.27 s before the ice.

3. GORE
None in this video. No blood, wounds, bodies or dismemberment appear at all, so there is nothing to describe here. Gore references have to come from videos 2-5.

What does carry over is how ground marks are handled:
- After the lightning, a black star-shaped scorch decal with glowing yellow cracks stays at least 0.5 s (still visible at 14.93 s and 15.07 s).
- After the slash, a pink "X" scar stays on the pad and fades slowly from about 16.2 s to about 17.3 s (roughly 1 s).
- The same approach (a decal that stays, then fades) suits blood splats and pools.

4. SOUND CUES (inferred from the visuals) AND PACING
Likely sound sync points, from the brightest frames:
- 0.20 s, 3.27 s, 7.33 s: big booms or shatters.
- 14.20 s: lightning crack.
- 15.80 s, 17.80 s, 18.80 s: slash "shing" plus impact.

Each impact seems to have a short build-up before it (spark crawl, glowing seed), which would carry a rising "whoosh" or charge sound.

Pacing:
- The camera moves on to a new effect about every 1.5-3 s.
- Each effect follows: a short build-up (0.1-0.6 s), a 1-3 frame white flash, an instant pop to full size, a 1-2 s hold with secondary motion, then a quick 0.3 s breakup into particles.
- Intensity is constant and high. It is a showcase reel, not gameplay.

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS (actionable, adapted to a realistic gore fighter)
- Every impact gets a 1-3 frame (about 0.07-0.2 s) overexposed white/bloom flash at the contact point before anything else appears (0.20 s, 3.27 s, 7.33 s, 14.20 s). For our punches: a 1-2 frame bright, slightly warm flash plus a small hitstop on the same frame.
- Impact effects pop in at full size in 0-1 frames rather than growing in. The crystals are full size within 1 frame of the flash at 7.40 s, and the X-slash at 15.80 s. Only secondary layers grow over time.
- Each impact is several layers fired together: a core flash, a radial thin-line spark starburst (about 4 frames), a ground shockwave ring that rolls outward (about 0.8 s, at 3.80-4.67 s), and thrown debris chunks.
- Use a brief build-up (0.1-0.6 s) before big hits: ground spark crawl or a glowing seed. For heavy or finisher attacks this maps to a wind-up glow or dust kick, plus a camera push-in.
- Blade or swing trails are thin crescents. The trail crosses the target in 2-3 frames, then lingers and thins for about 0.3 s after the hit (15.60-16.13 s). Use this for fast arcing strikes, but in a muted realistic colour, not pink.
- Ground marks stay after the hit. Scorch decals last 0.5 s or more and the slash scar fades over about 1 s. Do the same for blood: decals and pools should stay many seconds and fade slowly, never vanish instantly.
- Effects end by breaking up with dark noise erosion and a burst of small shards (2.33-2.67 s). They never just vanish. Blood mist or dust clouds should erode the same way and shed droplets.
- Debris has weight. Ice shards tumble and fall over about 1 s (7.47-8.40 s), and rock chunks are thrown out of the explosion. For gore: droplets, teeth and chunks should follow ballistic arcs and land.
- Hold-to-dissolve proportions to copy: about 10% build-up and flash, about 70% hold with secondary motion, about 20% fast breakup.
- Do NOT copy the toon look: black outlines, flat orange or pastel colours, and big magic shapes clash with a gritty realistic setting. Keep the timing; switch to realistic materials (dark red viscous blood, grey-brown dust, small sparks only on metal).
- This video has no reference for character animation, hit reactions or gore. Those must come from the other videos.

Unsure: the exact amount of screen shake (it is mixed with the orbiting camera), and whether any brief camera-shake frames are deliberate.
