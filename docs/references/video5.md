VIDEO 5: "BEST TSB ANIMATIONS PT 1" / "GRAND FISSURE FINISHER" (16 s, vertical TikTok edit of The Strongest Battlegrounds or a TSB-like game)

Short version: this video contains NO gore at all. No blood, no dismemberment, no damage numbers beyond a small "7%" tag. It is a stylized anime aura finisher (purple dragon, cel-shaded outlines). For a realistic, gritty gore game it is useful for one thing: the TIMING, CAMERA and IMPACT GRAMMAR (anticipation, white flash frame, radial speed lines, crater/crack decals, multi-hit barrage, launch into a wall). It is not useful for the colour palette or the anime "spirit dragon" effects. Two clips:

Clip A, 0.00-2.80 s: an idle/emote "drink can" animation (orange R6 avatar, white clothes). No combat.
- 0.00-0.53: the character flips a can into the air (it spins up above the head), with a light hop and alternating legs. Loose, bouncy body motion with plenty of overlap.
- 0.60-1.13: catches it, extends the arm forward holding the can (a "cheers"/show pose), with a weight shift on the hips.
- 1.20-2.80: brings it to the chest and drinks, with small knee bobs. The idle never goes fully still.
- Takeaway: the idle/emote animations feel alive because the hips and knees keep moving (weight shifts every ~0.3-0.5 s) and the props have their own arcs. Nothing here about attacks.

Clip B, 2.87-16.00 s: "Grand Fissure" finisher (attacker in a dark purple aura vs a black-suited "Weakest Dummy" R6 rig).

Hit reactions: the target never staggers softly. Every reaction is extreme: an instant launch (target off screen in 2-3 frames), being embedded in a wall (pinned while hit), then a limp ragdoll fall. Knockback is enormous and instant, with no ease-in.

2. HIT VFX
- Impact frame: 1 frame of full-screen white (3.80) or a black-silhouette frame (6.73). Each is the very first frame of contact.
- Radial speed lines (white, sometimes black) framing the hit point on big hits (6.73, 11.27, 12.07).
- Jagged star-shaped spark bursts (white plus a coloured accent) for barrage hits, 1-2 frames each, rotated randomly per hit.
- A circular "disc" flash with a thin rim for heavy single hits (4.53, 6.80), lasting 2 frames.
- Concentric expanding shockwave rings after the final hit (11.33-11.73), growing outward and fading over ~6-7 frames.
- Ground/wall CRACK DECALS: spider-web black cracks spawned on the impact frame, which scale up with each harder hit (6.87 small, 8.53 whole wall), plus a darker crater center. Persistent.
- Dust/smoke: a grey volumetric smoke wave after the launch (3.93-4.27), a dust ring on landing (13.40), and a dust streak left on the wall.
- Screen shake / camera: a camera tilt plus a slight shake on big hits; cuts to new angles at each major beat (ground, sky, wall, close-up). Also a zoom-in/FOV punch at 11.27 (the frame is tighter, with a vignette).
- Colour accent: the base hit is white, with purple as the move's colour, and a RED core reserved for the final/killing hit (11.27). That red-on-final distinction is the most transferable idea.
- UI: only a small red "7%" label above the dummy and the "Weakest Dummy" name tag. No big damage numbers or hit markers. The move bar at the bottom shows "DRAGON'S DESCENT" (an ultimate meter).

3. GORE
- None visible. No blood (no sprays, no droplets, no pools, no decals), no dismemberment, no wounds. The "damage" is shown entirely through environment destruction (cracks/craters), ragdoll and VFX. If the reference is meant for gore, this video does not show it; use the other reference videos for that.
- Body after the final hit: a limp ragdoll slumped against the cracked wall, staying in place until the end of the clip (~1 s+). Not removed.

4. SOUND (inferred) / PACING
- Big bass impacts would land exactly on the white/black flash frames: 3.80, 4.33, 6.73, 8.47, 11.27, 12.07. The silence/pause at 10.80-11.20 sets up the final hit.
- The barrage at 9.00-10.73 implies a rapid punch SFX loop at ~8-10 hits/s.
- Pacing: windup (1 s) -> hit -> combo of multiple escalating beats (each beat bigger than the previous one, every ~1.5-2 s) -> barrage -> pause -> final hit (red) -> body falls -> fade. Very high intensity, with ~13 s for one finisher (it is a showcase ultimate, far too long for a normal attack).

5. KEY TAKEAWAYS (actionable, adapted to a realistic gritty game)
- On the exact contact frame of a heavy hit, flash 1 frame (white, or a dark silhouette/inverted frame), then show the spark/debris on the next 1-2 frames. Never ease into an impact.
- Use a long, readable anticipation for heavy moves (~10-13 frames crouch/windup), then an instant (0-1 frame) strike. The contrast sells the power.
- Knockback should be instant and big: the target accelerates to full speed on the first frame after contact (leaves frame in 2-3 frames when launched). No soft float.
- Wall/floor impacts spawn a crack decal on the SAME frame the body touches the surface, scaled to hit strength (small web for normal hits, large crater for finishers); keep it persistent (at least several seconds).
- Keep barrage hits at ~8-10 hits/s, with each hit getting a 1-2 frame spark of random rotation/size and micro camera shake; leave a 4-6 frame silence/hold before the final hit.
- Make the killing or final hit look different from the others (here a red core flash plus expanding rings plus FOV punch). In a gore game, this is where the blood burst/dismemberment should go.
- Use expanding shockwave rings (2-3, ~6 frames, fading out) and a dust ring for ground impacts and landings.
- After death, switch the body to a limp ragdoll that slides down/settles against walls with a dust trail, and leave it in place.
- Change camera angles on major beats and add a short tilt/shake on heavy contact; keep the camera locked during fast barrages so hits stay readable.
- Damage UI stays minimal (small % tag); feedback comes from VFX, camera and environment, not from numbers.
- DO NOT copy: the purple aura, the cel-shaded spirit dragon, the anime star bursts. They clash with a realistic style. Translate them into realistic equivalents: white/neutral flash, dust, debris chunks, concrete cracks, blood (from the other references).
- Unsure: exact hitstop length. The frames at 15 fps suggest 1-2 frame holds on big impacts, but sub-frame freezes cannot be measured at this frame rate.
